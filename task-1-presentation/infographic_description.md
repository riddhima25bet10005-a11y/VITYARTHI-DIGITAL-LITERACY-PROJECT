# Task 1 – Digital Literacy Awareness Infographic

## Tool Used
**Google Slides with a SlidesCarniVal template** (slidescarnival.com) was used to design the infographic. SlidesCarniVal provides free, professionally designed templates that can be imported directly into Google Slides. The tool is fully browser-based, requires no installation, and allows easy export to image or PDF format.

## What the Design Shows
The infographic is titled **"Be Smart Online — A Digital Literacy Guide"** and is structured around four main sections plus two supplementary strips:

1. **Top Digital Tools for Students** – Five free, browser-based tools (Google Drive, Canva, Grammarly, Notion, Zoom) every first-year student should know
2. **Email Etiquette – Do It Right** – Six rules for writing professional emails to professors and recruiters, including a clear warning against using slang or emoji in formal communication
3. **Safe Internet Practices** – Six practical safety habits including OTP protection, HTTPS verification, and 2FA
4. **Build Your Professional Presence** – Four platforms (LinkedIn, GitHub, Kaggle, Stack Overflow) with brief descriptions of each
5. **4 Steps to Stay Safe Online** – A step-by-step visual flow: Think → Verify → Protect → Report
6. **Quick Tips Strip** – Six bite-sized facts every student should know, including the UPI QR code misconception and the national helpline 1930

## Reflection
The most challenging part of this task was making the infographic informative without making it look cluttered. I chose a blue-and-white colour palette with yellow accents to keep the design clean and readable. The decision to add the "4 Steps" flow at the bottom was something I found interesting — turning a list of safety tips into a visual process made it feel more actionable. I found Google Slides surprisingly capable for infographic design once a good template was applied.
